Standups, as practiced at most companies, are a meeting designed for the
person who doesn't need them. Three sentences from each engineer, in
sequence, about yesterday and today, while everyone waits for their turn.
The information is shallow, the format is rigid, and the only person
actually listening is the manager looking for someone to flag.

I think there's a better version. I've been running it on my own teams for
a few years and I built a small tool to make it easier.

## The format

The version I use is async, written, and structured around three questions:

1. **What did I ship?** Not what I worked on. What's actually merged or
   visible to a user.
2. **What's blocking me?** If anything. If nothing is blocking you, say so —
   that's useful information too.
3. **What am I focused on next?** One thing. The thing that matters most.

That's it. Three questions, written down, by 10am.

## Why it works

Three reasons, in order of importance.

**It respects the writer's time.** Nobody is sitting in a meeting waiting
for their turn. Engineers write their standup when they're already in
flow-state planning mode for the day, not at an arbitrary 9:30am.

**It respects the reader's time.** Managers (and curious teammates) can
read everyone's standup in two minutes, scan for blockers, and move on.
You can also actually search standups from three weeks ago, which is
something you cannot do with a meeting.

**It makes blockers visible without forcing performance.** People are much
more willing to write "I'm stuck on X" than they are to say it out loud
in front of seven coworkers. The async format lowers the activation energy
for asking for help, which is the most valuable signal a standup can
surface.

## The tool

I built [Stellar Standups](https://stellarstandups.com) because the
existing options were either bloated SaaS products or Slack bots that I
didn't trust to outlive their funding round.

It's a small Laravel app. It does one thing: gives a team a URL where they
can submit their standup and read everyone else's. No notifications, no
"productivity insights," no AI summaries. The app is open source and you
can self-host it if you want.

## What I'd change

The thing I'd add, if I were building it for the first time today, is a
weekly digest — an automatic rollup of what each person shipped that week,
suitable for sharing with non-engineering stakeholders. The information is
already in the standups; surfacing it would take an afternoon.

The thing I'd remove, if I could go back, is the calendar view. Nobody uses
it. It's beautiful and useless, like a lot of features.
